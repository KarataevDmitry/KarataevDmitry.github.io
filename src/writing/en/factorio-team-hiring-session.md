An interview where someone draws boxes for two hours and talks about the garbage collector says little about **how they will work next to you**. A team is not a roster of solo heroes. It is the ability to see neighbors’ strengths and weaknesses and arrange work so that **one person’s strengths cover another’s gaps**.

There is a format that tests exactly that — without a thousand dull questions. It does not have to be Factorio, but Factorio fits surprisingly well.

## What hiring actually needs

At work you rarely need “the best algorithmist in a vacuum.” You need people who:

- spot bottlenecks before everything stops;
- hand off a slice of work without ego;
- re-plan after conflict instead of revenge-refactoring;
- hold a **sector**, not a “pretty corner” while the factory starves.

The usual pipeline measures **reconstruction of the past** and **solo performance under anxiety**. A shared mission under time pressure measures **coordination under shared mutable state** — closer to production, and more honest.

## Factorio as a probe (not a “gamer interview”)

Factorio is not a test of memorized recipes. It is a **real-time behavioral interview**:

| Visible in one–two days | Invisible on a whiteboard |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Coordination when a line breaks | A beautiful REST API sketch |
| Taking vs. micromanaging someone else’s zone | A brilliant “design Twitter” |
| Escalating “there is a gap, need a human” | A story about conflict resolution three years ago |
| Thinking about team throughput | Thinking about perfect belt layout |

**Day 1** you can still play “I’m smart.” **Day 2** exposes the map: who is logistics, power, research, **firefighter reserve**.

## A military metaphor (not decoration)

A Factorio team resembles **sector defense with shared resources**:

- **Field of fire** — where someone is genuinely strong.
- **Overlapping coverage** — their skill closes a neighbor’s gap.
- **Blind spots** — a bottleneck nobody owned because “not my job.”
- **Mobile reserve** — not buried in one line; can jump where it burns.
- **Radio** — explicit coordination: “I take oil, don’t touch it.”

A strong team is not “five fullstack heroes” but **complementarity + handoff protocol**. A solo genius without coordination hits science-pack starvation before showing genius.

## The main point: not “playing under observation”

Bad version: HR and a lead watch through glass; **one person** decides for everyone.

Good version:

```
one–two days on a shared mission (Factorio or equivalent)
        ↓
full-team retro (+ candidate in the room)
        ↓
“So, folks — what do you think? Hire / pass / one more day?”
        ↓
decision = team consensus, not a single lead’s verdict
```

**Observation is unnecessary** — the signal is already inside the team. Who talked to whom, who jumped to a fire, who offered a handoff, who stayed silent in a blind zone — every participant saw it.

The candidate **at the retro** is a strong move. They hear not “we’ll call you back” but specifics: “we lacked X,” “you nailed Y,” “on the oil line you did Z — we’re ok / not ok with that.” Respect and clarity together.

## Four birds, one format

1. **Dull interviews** — drop the theater of “prove intelligence in a vacuum.” Skills show in action.
2. **Team fit** — not “do we drink beer together” but feedback, ownership, complementarity, behavior under breakage.
3. **Skills fit** — logistics / power / firefighter roles appear without resume keywords.
4. **Motivated reject/accept** — a team that **itself** said “want / don’t want” is already co-invested. A reject with named reasons at retro beats HR lottery.

## Guardrails (so it doesn’t become “hire only our friends”)

- **Criteria before play** — not “liked them,” but 3–4 axes: coordination, ownership, feedback, adaptability. Retro against those.
- **Different roles in the session** — scout, logistics lead, firefighter — so you’re not filtering “non–Factorio players” but behavior in a role.
- **Minority voice** — one “against” is not an automatic veto, but **requires a stated reason** at retro. Otherwise groupthink.
- **Optional track** — for people uncomfortable with RTS, the same principle (shared mission + retro + team decision) on another format. Not a gate for everyone.

## What this is not

- Not a replacement for all stages (legal, compliance, deep domain stay).
- Not “hire only Factorio obsessives.”
- Not a guarantee: one weekend does not replace months of work together — but it beats a fifth system-design round on a board.

## In one paragraph

**A team** is a map without blind spots, overlapping sectors, and a reserve that arrives on time. Factorio (or any shared mission with mutable state) shows that map faster than “tell me about the GC.” A **team decision at retro with the candidate present** makes accept and reject motivated — for both sides.

A weekend hiring session beats another whiteboard URL shortener.
